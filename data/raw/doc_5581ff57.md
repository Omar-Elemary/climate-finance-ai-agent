# Paris Climate Agreement
Last updated: 
January 27, 2026
Actions 
Rescinded 
![](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/23202759664_f6de66e6e0_c.jpg)
## Current Status
On Jan. 7, 2026, President Trump announced that the [U.S. will withdraw](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/withdrawing-the-united-states-from-international-organizations-conventions-and-treaties-that-are-contrary-to-the-interests-of-the-united-states/) from the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). On Jan. 27, 2026, the United States [withdrawal from the Paris Agreement](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/CN/2025/CN.71.2025-Eng.pdf) became effective after a year-long process.
## Why It Matters
The United States is the [second largest greenhouse gas emitter in the world](https://www.epa.gov/ghgemissions/inventory-us-greenhouse-gas-emissions-and-sinks) and the largest historical emitter. The US’s approval of the Paris Climate Agreement in 2016 signaled the country’s intention to cooperate globally in reducing our emissions. Approximately 200 countries have formally adopted the agreement. Under the first Trump administration, the US withdrew from the Paris Agreement. The Biden Administration rejoined on Feb. 19, 2021, and committed to achieving a 50 to 52 percent greenhouse gas emissions reduction from 2005 levels by 2030. In Jan. 2025, President Trump directed the UN Ambassador to withdraw from the Paris Agreement.
## Timeline
[Trump Administration II](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/tracker/paris-climate-agreement/)
**Jan. 27, 2026** The United States’ [withdrawal from the Paris Agreement](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/CN/2025/CN.71.2025-Eng.pdf) became effective on Jan. 27, 2026, following a year-long process [initiated by President Donald Trump](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/putting-america-first-in-international-environmental-agreements/) on the first day of his second term.
**Jan. 7, 2026** The Trump administration [announced](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/withdrawing-the-united-states-from-international-organizations-conventions-and-treaties-that-are-contrary-to-the-interests-of-the-united-states/) that it is withdrawing the United States from the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), the treaty that underlies international cooperation on climate change. Since it was established in 1992, 198 countries around the globe have joined.
**Jan. 20, 2025** The Trump administration by [executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/putting-america-first-in-international-environmental-agreements/) announced the United States will withdraw from the Paris Accords. The terms of the treaty make the withdrawal effective one year after the filing a notice to withdraw. At that point, the United States will join Libya, Yemen, and Iran as the only non-signatories to the treaty.
[Biden Administration](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/tracker/paris-climate-agreement/)
**November 11-24, 2024** The [United Nations Climate Change Conference, COP29 was held in Baku, Azerbaijan](https://unfccc.int/news/cop29-un-climate-conference-agrees-to-triple-finance-to-developing-countries-protecting-lives-and). Critical outcomes of the conference included attendee nations ratifying a framework under Article 6.4 of the [Paris Agreement](https://www.google.com/search?q=paris+agreement&rlz=1C1GCEB_enUS1081US1081&oq=Paris+Agreement&gs_lcrp=EgZjaHJvbWUqCggAEAAYsQMYgAQyCggAEAAYsQMYgAQyCggBEAAYsQMYgAQyBwgCEAAYgAQyBwgDEAAYgAQyBwgEEAAYgAQyBwgFEAAYgAQyBwgGEAAYgAQyBwgHEAAYgAQyBwgIEAAYgAQyBwgJEAAYgATSAQo2MzQ2MGowajE1qAIIsAIB&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8) to create a UN-backed body to regulate international carbon credit trading and an agreement on a new climate finance pledge from developed nations to assist developing countries. The [New Collective Quantified Goal on Climate Finance (NCQG)](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/UNFCCC_NCQG2023_flyer_web.pdf) increased financial support pledges to $300 billion annually by 2035, replacing the previous goal of $100 billion annually.
**Dec. 13, 2023** 200 countries including the US signed an agreement at the climate summit in Dubai (COP28) to “transition[] away from fossil fuels in energy systems, in a just, orderly and equitable manner . . . so as to achieve net zero by 2050 in keeping with the science.” The agreement also calls for tripling global renewable energy capacity by 2030, and accelerating the use of carbon capture and storage technologies. [To read the latest version of the draft agreement and supporting documents, click here](https://unfccc.int/documents). Additional US commitments at COP28 include:
  * [The Lowering Organic Waste Methane (LOW-Methane)](https://www.state.gov/lowering-organic-waste-methane-initiative-low-methane/), a new initiative to reduce methane emissions from the waste sector. The United States, among other backers, will provide data and finance support to a target group of lower income countries seeking to slash their methane emissions. The initiative is intended to support the [Global Methane Pledge of cutting methane emissions at least 30% by 2030 relative to 2020 levels](https://www.globalmethanepledge.org/#pledges).
  * The Department of State, the Bezos Earth Fund, and the Rockefeller Foundation [launched the Energy Transition Accelerator (ETA)](https://www.state.gov/united-states-and-partners-announce-energy-transition-accelerator-framework/), [an initiative that will pay lower income countries for verified power sector emissions reductions and sell the resulting carbon credits to private businesses](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/initiative/energy-transition-accelerator/). The three pilot countries will be Chile, the Dominican Republic, and Nigeria.
  * The “Sapporo 5” of the United States, Canada, Japan, France, and the United Kingdom [announced an intent to expand nuclear fuel production by mobilizing $4.2 billion](https://www.energy.gov/articles/cop28-us-canada-france-japan-and-uk-announce-plans-mobilize-42-billion-reliable-global) of public and private funding over the next three years. The stated goal is to [increase the supply of zero-carbon nuclear energy](https://www.state.gov/the-united-states-announces-key-measures-to-jump-start-deployments-of-advanced-nuclear-energy-systems-and-to-secure-nuclear-fuel-supply-chains-accelerating-the-contribution-of-nuclear-energy-to-net-z/) in the five partner countries.
  * The US [joined the Freshwater Challenge](https://www.state.gov/u-s-support-for-water-security-as-a-climate-adaptation-priority/), an international initiative intended [to accelerate the restoration of 300,000 km of degraded rivers and nearly 865 million acres of degraded wetlands by 2030](https://www.freshwaterchallenge.org/).


**Nov. 18, 2023** The Article 6.4 Supervisory Body, the United Nations working group tasked to operationalize a UN carbon crediting mechanism under the Paris Agreement, [agreed on a set of recommendations](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/a64-sb009-a02.pdf). The recommendations are intentionally broad and technology-neutral, stating that “[projects that remove greenhouse gasses from the atmosphere and destroy or durably store them](https://unfccc.int/news/un-body-agrees-vital-carbon-crediting-guidance-ahead-of-cop28)” will generate carbon credits. The eventual [goal is to facilitate international trade in carbon removal credits](https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/energy-transition/111923-cop28-agreement-reached-on-carbon-market-framework). The recommendations will next go to the Parties to the Paris Agreement (CMA) for approval at COP28, which runs from Nov. 30 to Dec. 12, 2023 in Dubai.
**June 23, 2023**[ The Summit for a New Global Financing Pact](https://nouveaupactefinancier.org/en.php) is hosted in Paris and discussions include climate finance for developing countries. 
**April 20, 2023** While convening the Major Economies Forum on Energy and Climate, President Biden [encourages world leaders to adopt targets](https://perma.cc/A8FR-P4QN) for 2030 emissions levels consistent with a 1.5 °C warming goal. Biden [announces](https://perma.cc/3LFM-24F6) a $1 billion U.S. commitment to the Green Climate Fund, which finances renewable energy and climate resilience projects in developing countries; a “Carbon Management Challenge” urging nations to put forward “concrete commitments” relating to carbon capture and removal technology ahead of COP28; a “Methane Finance Sprint” aimed to raise funds for developing countries’ methane emissions reduction; and a plan to request funding from Congress to prevent deforestation in the Amazon.
**Nov. 20, 2022**[ Diplomats from nearly 200 countries reached an agreement to form a fund to compensate vulnerable countries for the adverse effects of climate change](https://unfccc.int/news/cop27-reaches-breakthrough-agreement-on-new-loss-and-damage-fund-for-vulnerable-countries), capping off more than two weeks of negotiations at COP27. The deal, which does little to facilitate emissions reduction, provides for the creation of a 24-country committee that will be tasked with determining the fund’s form and how money will be gathered and allocated. The United States and other developed countries had for decades opposed such a loss and damage deal.
**Nov. 11, 2022**[ The Biden Administration announces a number of new climate policies](https://perma.cc/WN8L-MKS5), including [increased financial contributions to global adaptation and resilience efforts](https://perma.cc/7CAS-8556), [updated domestic standards for regulating methane emissions](https://perma.cc/DA83-2Z5B), and [a proposed rule that would require federal government suppliers to publicly disclose their climate-related risks](https://perma.cc/H8H7-4V5N). 
**Nov. 11, 2022**[ President Biden addresses the Conference](https://perma.cc/N3E2-KKKU), affirming his administration’s commitment to achieving its emissions reduction goal of a 50 to 52 percent reduction below 2005 levels by 2030, as set forth in the Paris Agreement. 
**Nov. 6, 2022** [The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change convenes for its 27th Conference of the Parties (COP27) in Sharm El Sheikh, Egypt](https://perma.cc/23U3-ALYG).
**Aug, 16, 2022** President Biden signs the [Inflation Reduction Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/5376) into law, which will move the US closer to its emissions reduction goals under the Paris Agreement. The bill is designed to spur investment in clean energy through tax incentives, grants, and other funding mechanisms. The law will drive investment of nearly $369 billion in clean energy and climate priorities, which in turn is [projected to reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions](https://rhg.com/research/inflation-reduction-act/) by about 40 percent below 2005 levels by 2030 (relative to 30 percent under current policy).
**Nov. 13, 2021** COP26 [concludes](https://unfccc.int/news/cop26-reaches-consensus-on-key-actions-to-address-climate-change) in Glasgow after two weeks. The negotiations focused on adaptation, mitigation, and global financing. Additional outcomes [include](https://www.wri.org/insights/cop26-key-outcomes-un-climate-talks-glasgow):
  * 151 countries, including the US, [submit](https://www.climatewatchdata.org/2020-ndc-tracker) new nationally determined contributions (NDCs). The UN calculates that these plans put the world on track for 2.5 degrees C of warming by the end of the century. The Glasgow decision calls on countries to “revisit and strengthen” their 2030 targets by the end of 2022 to align them with the Paris Agreement’s goal of 1.5 degrees C of warming.
  * The Parties recommit to provide $100 billion annually in climate finance from developed to developing nations, starting in 2023. Countries did not meet the existing target to provide this money starting in 2020.
  * Parties agree on a rulebook to govern “Article 6” international carbon markets, including norms to prevent double counting of emission reductions. However, the rules also allow the carry-over of old carbon credits generated since 2013 under the Kyoto Protocol. For more on Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, see additional resources [here](https://www.edf.org/climate/implementing-paris-climate-agreement).
  * Parties agree to an Enhanced Transparency Framework to account for and report targets and emissions.


**Nov. 10, 2021** The US and China [announce](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/11/10/climate/climate-cop26-glasgow.html?name=styln-cop26&region=TOP_BANNER&block=storyline_menu_recirc&action=click&pgtype=Article&variant=show&is_new=false) a joint agreement to “enhance ambition” on climate change, with China committing to reduce methane emissions for the first time. However, the agreement does not include an emissions ceiling or timetable for China to reduce its emissions.
**Nov. 7, 2021** In the first week of the COP26 conference, the US delegation takes a number of different climate actions including making commitments to: 
  * [Reduce](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-59137828) methane emissions 30 percent by 2030 compared with 2020 levels. As part of the US’s commitment, EPA issues a [proposed](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/us-sharply-cut-methane-pollution-threatens-climate-and-public-health) rule to regulate both new and existing sources of methane in the oil and gas sector. (For more on the proposed rule, see EELP’s [Regulatory Tracker page](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/2019/09/epa-voc-and-methane-standards-for-oil-and-gas-facilities/)). Over 100 countries joined the pledge; China, India, and Russia did not. 
  * [Provide](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2021/11/01/fact-sheet-president-biden-renews-u-s-leadership-on-world-stage-at-u-n-climate-conference-cop26/) $3 billion in climate adaptation financing for developing nations per year starting in FY 2024 as part of the President’s Emergency Plan for Adaptation and Resilience ([PREPARE](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Full-PREPARE-Plan.pdf)) initiative. The funding will need to be approved by Congress.
  * [End and reverse deforestation](https://ukcop26.org/glasgow-leaders-declaration-on-forests-and-land-use/) worldwide by 2030. Of the $19.2 billion pledged in private and public funds, the US commits $9 billion to conserve and restore forests. The signing countries together represent roughly 85% of the world’s forest-covered land, including Brazil, Russia, Canada, and Indonesia. 


The US does not join 40 other nations that [commit](https://ukcop26.org/global-coal-to-clean-power-transition-statement/) to phase out coal, including India and China. 
**Oct. 14, 2021** President Biden [announces](https://www.cnn.com/2021/10/14/politics/biden-cabinet-members-attending-cop26-climate/index.html) a team of 12 cabinet members and high-level officials that will attend the COP26 talks, including White House climate advisor Gina McCarthy, US climate envoy John Kerry, EPA administrator Michael Regan, and Secretary of State Anthony Blinken.
**April 22, 2021** The US hosts a virtual [Leaders Summit on Climate](https://www.state.gov/leaders-summit-on-climate/) and the Biden administration [commits](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2021/04/22/fact-sheet-president-biden-sets-2030-greenhouse-gas-pollution-reduction-target-aimed-at-creating-good-paying-union-jobs-and-securing-u-s-leadership-on-clean-energy-technologies/) to reducing US greenhouse gas emissions by 50 to 52 percent in 2030 from 2005 levels.
**Feb. 19, 2021** The US [rejoined](https://www.state.gov/the-united-states-officially-rejoins-the-paris-agreement/) the Paris Agreement.
**Jan. 27, 2021** President Biden, in [EO 14008](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/02/01/2021-02177/tackling-the-climate-crisis-at-home-and-abroad), instructs the government to immediately begin developing a new Nationally Determined Contribution to be released at a Leaders’ Climate Summit being planned for April.
**Jan. 20, 2021** President Biden [formally accepts the Agreement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2021/01/20/paris-climate-agreement/), initiating the process to rejoin the Paris Agreement.
[Trump Administration I](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/tracker/paris-climate-agreement/)
**Nov. 4, 2020** The United States [officially withdraws](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/11/04/climate/paris-climate-agreement-trump.html) from the Paris Agreement.
**Nov. 4, 2019** The Trump Administration [formally notifies](https://www.npr.org/2019/11/04/773474657/u-s-formally-begins-to-leave-the-paris-climate-agreement) the United Nations that the U.S. will withdraw from the Paris Agreement. The withdrawal process will take one year, making withdrawal effective on Nov. 4, 2020.
**Feb. 1, 2018** The [European Commission announces](https://thinkprogress.org/eu-paris-us-decd4aad9145/) the European Union will not sign trade agreements with countries that did not ratify the Paris Climate Agreement.
**June 1, 2017**[President Trump](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/trump-to-announce-us-will-exit-paris-climate-deal/2017/06/01/fbcb0196-46da-11e7-bcde-624ad94170ab_story.html?utm_term=.2518144c206c)[ announces](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/trump-to-announce-us-will-exit-paris-climate-deal/2017/06/01/fbcb0196-46da-11e7-bcde-624ad94170ab_story.html?utm_term=.2518144c206c) the United States will withdraw from the Paris Climate Agreement.
[Obama Administration](https://eelp.law.harvard.edu/tracker/paris-climate-agreement/)
**Nov. 4, 2016** The [Paris Climate Agreement](https://unfccc.int/paris_agreement/items/9444.php) on greenhouse gas emissions mitigation, adaptation, and finance [enters into force ](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/CN/2016/CN.735.2016-Eng.pdf)now that at least 55 nations have formally joined by submitting a plan for addressing climate change.
**April 22, 2016** The [United States signs](https://unfccc.int/paris_agreement/items/9444.php) the Paris Climate Agreement treaty and commits to reducing domestic greenhouse gas emissions by 26 to 28 percent compared with 2005 levels.
