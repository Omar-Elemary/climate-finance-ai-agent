# The energy transition
What do we mean by the energy transition? Why is it so important in combating climate change? What can help to accelerate it? And what role does electrification play in this process? Here’s everything that needs to be known about the most important challenge of this century.

## **What is the energy transition?**
The **energy transition** is the "the global energy sector’s shift from fossil-based systems of energy production and consumption – including oil, natural gas and coal – to renewable energy sources like wind and solar, in order to achieve zero carbon emissions by 2050" ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/en/research-insights/market-insights/what-is-energy-transition)).
It’s part of the international strategy adopted by governments to limit global warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.
### **Why it’s important**
According to data provided by [Copernicus](https://climate.copernicus.eu/global-climate-highlights-2024#:~:text=2024%20had%20a%20global%20average,exceed%201.5%20above%20that%20level.) (“Europe’s eyes on Earth”), 2024 was the warmest year on record and the first to see an increase in the global average annual temperature of more than 1.5 degrees (Celsius) with respect to pre-industrial levels.
This is a dramatic figure, because [the 2015 Paris Agreement on climate change](https://www.un.org/en/climatechange/paris-agreement) aims to limit the long-term temperature increase (i.e., over a ten-year average, rather than a single year) to no more than 1.5°C.
In December 2023, [_COP28_](https://unfccc.int/news/cop28-agreement-signals-beginning-of-the-end-of-the-fossil-fuel-era)[in Dubai](https://unfccc.int/news/cop28-agreement-signals-beginning-of-the-end-of-the-fossil-fuel-era) concluded with an explicit agreement to **end the use of fossil fuels** , but it didn’t set specific targets for phasing them out completely, while acknowledging that countries around the world are not yet on track to meet the goal of limiting the global temperature rise to 1.5°C.
At _COP 29_ in Azerbaijan in 2024, consensus was reached on the establishment of an **international compensation fund** into which developed countries would pay $300 billion annually until 2035 for the benefit of developing economies.
The goal is still **carbon neutrality** by 2050, i.e., reducing and avoiding greenhouse gas emissions, offsetting the remaining emissions through the use of carbon credits.
### **Historical perspective**
The ongoing energy transition process is nothing new in terms of the evolution of humanity. In the past, we witnessed other major epochal shifts, such as the transition from wood to coal in the 19th century and from coal to oil in the 20th century.
What distinguishes this transition from previous ones is **the urgent need to protect the planet** from the greatest threat it has ever faced, and to do so as quickly as possible.
This impetus has accelerated change in the energy sector by speeding up the growth and spread of renewables. 
However, the energy transition is not limited to the gradual closure of coal-fired power plants and the development of clean energy: **it’s a paradigm shift for the entire system**.

## Actions in favor of the energy transition
We’re talking about a genuine paradigm shift. On the one hand, it requires the replacement of fossil fuels with **renewable** sources and, on the other, the **development of new technologies** such as [storage](https://www.enel.com/learning-hub/storage.html) and [hydrogen](https://www.enel.com/learning-hub/renewables/hydrogen.html), the electrification of certain sectors, and digitalization.
The development of renewable energy lies at the heart of the energy transition. In recent years, photovoltaics and wind power have joined mature technologies such as [hydroelectric](https://www.enel.com/learning-hub/renewables/hydroelectric-energy.html) and geothermal power and have established themselves as the major players in the ongoing transition.
This is a decisive shift focused on **decarbonization** , i.e., the gradual abandonment of fossil fuels and, therefore, the decommissioning of coal-fired power plants.
The **development of new sectors** , such as marine energy and green hydrogen, could also contribute to the transition in the near future, while storage **technologies** , i.e., energy storage systems that are capable of compensating for the intermittency of sources such as the Sun and wind, will be decisive.
The **electrification** of**consumption** and certain sectors, as well as the **digitalization of industrial processes and grids** , are also contributing to improving energy efficiency and completing the energy transition process.

### The economy and society
## **The energy transition for the economy and society**
The benefits **are not only for the environment**. The development of renewables and the conversion of old coal-fired power plants help the economy and create new jobs. The important thing is that no one is left behind.
The energy transition has become necessary in order to save our planet from the effects of climate change, but it would be a mistake to think that the benefits it produces are only environmental. Renewable sources and electrification certainly reduce pollution and improve air quality (just think of electric mobility), but the paradigm shift brought about by the energy transition is also a **great opportunity** in terms of economic well-being, employment growth, the social development of the communities involved, and improvement in the quality of life of people around the world today and for future generations.
### **The benefits for the economy and society**
A successful energy transition based on renewable energy is important because it offers benefits that go beyond solving the climate crisis, including:
  * **Creating new jobs** : up to 42 million new jobs by 2050, according to the United Nations; development of so-called _green jobs_ in the renewable energy, electric mobility, and energy efficiency sectors; compensation for jobs lost in the fossil fuel sectors, including the retraining and redeployment of personnel from old coal-fired power plants into new roles in the energy supply chain. 
  * **Promoting sustainable development** through the promotion of social equity and the reduction of inequalities, support for developing communities and local areas, stimulating the circular economy and sustainable business models, ensuring a _just transition_ that leaves no one behind, and strengthening the social fabric of local communities.
  * **Generating positive environmental and social impact** by reducing the natural disasters caused by climate change, reducing emissions by 45% by 2030 and to zero by 2050, protecting biodiversity and natural resources, and promoting their conservation;
  * **Improving** the health **of people and communities** : cleaner air can reduce pollution-related diseases. This goal can also be achieved through electrification (e.g., of mobility), which reduces air pollution, particularly in densely populated cities, with significant health benefits;
  * **Ensuring economic and financial benefits** , such as reducing costs for households and businesses in the long term, developing new investment opportunities in green technology, saving on fossil fuel subsidies ([calculated by the United Nations Development Programme](https://www.undp.org/thailand/press-releases/every-dollar-pledged-tackle-climate-crisis-worlds-poor-four-dollars-are-spent-fossil-fuel-subsidies-keep-climate-crisis-alive) at approximately $423 billion per year, to be reinvested in public services) and price stability, with less dependence on fossil fuel markets and geopolitical dynamics**.** This is in addition to greater energy security and resilience to international market shocks;
  * **Combating energy poverty, which is** still widespread in many parts of the world, and ensuring universal access to clean, reliable, and affordable energy, creating development opportunities for local communities and areas;
  * **Promoting the conversion** of existing energy sites (such as coal-fired power plants) with renewable and innovation projects, leveraging existing infrastructure.
  * **Updating and retraining** operational staff at thermoelectric plants, leveraging their _know-how_ and creating new skills in terms of digitalization and the use of new tools such as Artificial Intelligence. 


Overall, the impact of the energy transition goes far beyond the environment, and affects almost all aspects of society. That’s why it’s essential that the energy transition is inclusive and leaves no one behind. In other words, it must be a _just transition_.
